# Watching five people use Husky Eye View (step 6)

Five sessions find most of the problems a first-time user hits. The rule:
**give a task, then stay quiet.** Every time you would have helped is a
finding.

## Who

Five people from the main audience: graduate students or instructors in
sociology, public health, urban studies or economics who use Stata, R or
Excel, and who have **not** seen Husky Eye View. Mix: at least one who rarely
uses maps, at least one on a laptop (not a large monitor).

Before recruiting, ask Northeastern's IRB whether these sessions need review.
Product feedback that is not published as research usually does not, but the
IRB decides.

## Setup (before each session)

- A clean browser profile, Husky Eye View freshly started from Pinokio, the
  first-run card showing (add `?welcome=1` to the address).
- A practice file for task 3 on the desktop: a CSV of about 20 places with
  latitude and longitude (for example, community health centers).
- Screen and voice recording, with the participant's consent.
- A timer, and the observation sheet below.

## Script

> Thanks for helping. We are testing the software, not you; anything that is
> hard is our problem to fix. Please think aloud: say what you are looking
> for, what you expect, and what surprises you. I will mostly stay quiet. You
> can stop at any time.

## Tasks (read one at a time; 5 minutes each, then move on)

1. **First look.** "You've just opened this for the first time. Show me what
   it is for." *(Watch: do they read the first-run card? which tile?)*
2. **A question about a place.** "Find out how life expectancy compares
   between two Boston neighborhoods of your choice."
3. **Your own data.** "Here is a file of clinics. Put them on the map, then
   find out how many are in tracts where poverty is above 20%."
4. **Then and now.** "Show how a 1930s redlined area compares with today."
5. **A statistic.** "Find out whether life expectancy and income are related
   across Boston's tracts, and save the result."
6. **Tidy up.** "This screen has more than you need for your work. Make it
   show only what you would use."

The two most useful columns: **where they got stuck**, in their words, and
**the words they used**. If they call it "upload" and the button says "ADD A
FILE", that is a finding.

## After the tasks (5 minutes)

- "What would you use this for?" "What would stop you from using it?"
- "What did you expect to be there that wasn't?"
- The System Usability Scale (ten questions, 1–5, standard wording); a score
  under 68 is below average.

## After all five sessions

1. List every stuck point; count how many of the five hit each.
2. Fix anything three or more people hit before anything else.
3. Record the time to first win (task 3) for each person: the goal is under
   five minutes.
