# Week 10. Space and time

## Goals
- Describe change over time on a map without overstating it.
- Use Time Lens to compare 1930s redlining with today's data.
- Connect historical policy to present-day patterns, carefully.

## Concept (25 min)
- Temporal patterns: season, weekday, hour, long trends. Small multiples vs animation.
- Historical context: HOLC grading, residential segregation, disinvestment. A pattern across 90 years is an association with many steps in between.
- Boundary change and comparability (week 3 revisited).

## Lab (50 min)
1. Turn on 1930s HOLC redlining. Open Time Lens and move the window to show tract life expectancy through it.
2. Pick a covered city. Record the grades of three areas and today's value for each.
3. Read one HOLC area description in full and note the language used.
4. If your instructor has granted access, view Chicago historical homicides, 1870-1930, beside 1900-1930 enumeration districts. Otherwise use the segregation layers (514 cities, 2000 to today).
5. Build a three-panel small-multiples figure from screenshots (1930, 1970 or 2000, today).

## Discussion
What can a historical map prove about cause, and what can it only suggest?

## Deliverable
Small-multiples figure and a 300-word interpretation that states one claim and one limit.

## ArcGIS Pro equivalent
Time slider, Swipe tool, Mosaic layouts.

## Gap note
Time Lens shows layer pairs, not arbitrary date filters on a CSV. For incident
timelines, summarize by month or year in Excel.
