# Week 6. Mapping points

## Goals
- Make a point map of incidents and read it critically.
- Recognize overplotting and why dots mostly show where people live.
- Use annotations to mark and explain places.

## Concept (25 min)
- Pin maps, graduated symbols, categorical symbols.
- Overplotting: stacked points hide counts; jitter and aggregation.
- Dots follow population, so a point map is a map of people before it is a map of crime.

## Lab (50 min)
1. Load the Miami-Dade homicide layer. Zoom from county to neighborhood and watch the pattern change.
2. Bring your week 5 CSV into HEV through Merge & Export preview (points are drawn as a dot per area for large files, outlines for few). Compare.
3. Use annotations (marks, polygons, routes) to outline two areas you think are clusters and label each with a reason.
4. Fly the camera to a street-level view of one annotated area. What can you see that the map cannot show (lighting, vacant lots, cameras)?
5. Save the view as a Share Link.

## Discussion
You are asked for a map showing "where crime is." What three questions do you ask first?

## Deliverable
Point map of one crime type in one city, two annotations, caption stating what the dots do not tell the reader.

## ArcGIS Pro equivalent
Symbology (Unique Values, Graduated Symbols), Add Graphics, bookmarks.

## Gap note
No automatic jitter or clustering by category control; discuss, then handle in a spreadsheet if desired.
