# Week 14. Storytelling and final presentations

## Goals
- Turn an analysis into a short, honest, reproducible map story.
- Use Scenes, Scene Director, Curated Flights and Share Links to present it.
- Present and give feedback.

## Concept (20 min)
- Structure: question, evidence, caveat, takeaway. One idea per scene.
- Honest titles, captions, and a data-and-limits slide.
- What to hand over: the story, the data, the scripts, the log.

## Lab (35 min)
1. Build three scenes in the scene editor, each with a camera shot, layers on, and a hold time. Add a narration or caption for each.
2. Play it back through Scene Director. Trim to 3 minutes.
3. Generate a Share Link and, if used, a Curated Flights PDF report with data and charts.
4. Package: share link, merged data, saved session scripts and logs, memo.

## Presentations (remaining time)
Five minutes plus questions. Peers complete a feedback form: clear question,
honest limits, readable design, reproducible.

## Deliverable
The final project package and memo (see README).

## ArcGIS Pro equivalent
StoryMaps, Layout view, Export Layout.

## Gap note
Curated Flights compares up to three cities; plan the story around that limit.

## Plain-language glossary

- **Attribute table**: the spreadsheet behind a map layer; one row per place.
- **Choropleth**: a map that shades areas by a value.
- **Datum / projection**: the model of the Earth and the method of flattening it.
- **FIPS / GEOID**: numeric codes that identify counties and tracts.
- **Geocode**: turn an address into coordinates.
- **Hot spot**: a place with more incidents than chance would give.
- **MAUP**: results change when you redraw the zones.
- **Rate**: a count divided by population, usually per 100,000.
- **Tract**: a census area of about 4,000 residents.
- **Vector / raster**: shapes with attributes vs a grid of cell values.
