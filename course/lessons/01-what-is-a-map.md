# Week 1. What is a map?

## Goals
- Explain that every map selects, simplifies and argues.
- Navigate HEV: layers, search, camera, base map, Share Links.
- State a mappable crime question.

## Concept (25 min)
- Maps as models: scale, selection, generalization. Snow's cholera map and the
  Chicago School's concentric-zone maps as two early crime-and-place arguments.
- Four questions for any map: Who made it? What was left out? What is the
  unit? What does it want me to conclude?

## Lab (50 min): HEV orientation
1. Start HEV (Pinokio one-click or `npm`, see root README). Open the globe at the national view.
2. Turn on three layers one at a time: tract life expectancy, HOLC redlining, trauma centers. Note what each shows at the national and at the city scale.
3. Use the Location bar to fly to a Northeastern campus city. Change the base map (satellite, street).
4. Click a feature on each layer and read its card. Record the fields.
5. Save a Share Link for a view you find interesting; paste it in the class doc.

## Discussion
Which of your three layers is a measurement, which a classification, and which
a decision someone made in 1935?

## Deliverable
Half page: a crime question for a city you choose, the unit of analysis it
implies (point, block, tract, city), and one thing a map could not tell you.

Opening a project, Catalog pane, Contents pane, bookmarks.
