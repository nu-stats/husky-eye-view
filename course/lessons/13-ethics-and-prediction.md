# Week 13. Ethics, bias and prediction

## Goals
- Identify how crime maps can harm people and places.
- Explain feedback loops in predictive policing.
- Apply a short ethics checklist to your own project.

## Concept (25 min)
- Records measure police activity as well as crime; predictions trained on them can repeat it.
- Redlining as a case of a map that shaped lives. Critical GIS and counter-mapping.
- Privacy: victims' addresses, small-cell disclosure, re-identification, data-use terms.

## Lab (50 min)
1. Read a HOLC area description and a modern risk-score description. Compare how each labels a place.
2. Take your draft project map. Apply the checklist below and revise.
3. Rewrite your map's title and caption so they describe neighborhoods without stigma.
4. Practice a two-minute talk: what the map shows, what it does not, and who could be affected.

## Ethics checklist
- [ ] Does the map identify any person or a small number of people?
- [ ] Is the unit large enough for the count to be safe to show?
- [ ] Is a rate or a count shown, and is it fair to the area?
- [ ] Would residents of this area recognize themselves in the title?
- [ ] Are the data's limits in the caption?
- [ ] Do the data terms allow this use?

## Deliverable
Revised draft map, completed checklist, and a 200-word reflection.
