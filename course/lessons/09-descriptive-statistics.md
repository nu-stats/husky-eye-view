# Week 9. Describing spatial data

## Goals
- Calculate and interpret mean, median, spread and percentiles for a map variable.
- Read a scatterplot and a correlation; separate correlation from cause.
- Run a simple regression and read the coefficient in plain words.
- Rerun a classmate's saved session (reproducibility check).

## Concept (25 min)
- Distribution first: histograms and skew; crime rates are right-skewed.
- Correlation, the ecological fallacy (tract patterns are not individual behavior).
- Regression as "average difference, holding other things fixed"; confounding.

## Lab (50 min)
1. Open the Analysis menu and choose R (or Excel). Load a layer's data for one state's tracts. Run summaries for life expectancy and one other variable.
2. Make a histogram and a scatterplot of two variables.
3. Fit a simple regression with the commands the panel allows. Interpret the slope in a sentence.
4. Use the plain-English entry if available, and compare it with the command it produced.
5. Find the saved session folder; swap scripts with a partner and reproduce their output.

## Discussion
Tracts with lower life expectancy also show more violence. List three explanations
that are not "one causes the other."

## Deliverable
Output log, a three-sentence interpretation, and the partner reproducibility note (matched / did not match, why).

## ArcGIS Pro equivalent
Summary Statistics, Chart tools, Ordinary Least Squares (Spatial Statistics toolbox).

## Gap note
The R panel permits a fixed list of functions for safety; confirm what is
available. Spatial regression is out of scope; mention it as the next step.
